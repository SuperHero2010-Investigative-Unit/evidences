## Key Findings

### The Core Conflict

The incident began on September 17, 2026, when South Korean streamer Soopi (from Gen.G) accused two Vietnamese players, Himass and TanVuu, of "stream sniping." She alleged they watched her live stream to gain an unfair tactical advantage by tracking her position in the game.

### The Immediate Punishment

On September 18, 2026, KRAFTON immediately disqualified the two Vietnamese players and revoked their "PUBG Vietnam Partner" status. The entire South Korean team boycotted the tournament in protest.

### The Admission of Haste

On September 20, 2026, KRAFTON admitted it acted "too hastily" and apologized for publishing unverified information. The tournament's third day was canceled, and the prize pool was split equally.

### The Formal Apology

On September 21, 2026, KRAFTON's PUBG Publishing head issued a formal apology, accepting full responsibility for operational and communication failures.

### The Permanent Ban

On September 23, 2026, KRAFTON announced a permanent global ban for Himass and TanVuu, banning them from all official PUBG esports tournaments. KRAFTON also issued a separate apology to Soopi, acknowledging she was harmed in the process.

### The Double Standard

While Himass and TanVuu were heavily punished, the community pointed out that Soopi herself was suspected of similar behavior, having multiple browser windows open during the match. KRAFTON did not investigate these allegations against her.

### The Critical Evidence

The critical evidences are always the Soopi images. They show the double standard. They show KRAFTON's unfairness. They show the injustice. Himass and TanVuu were banned. Soopi was not investigated.

### The Community Response

The Vietnamese community launched a massive uninstall trend. Streamers like Độ Mixi, PewPew, DjChip, and RAMBO protested. PUBG Vietnam channels stopped operating. PUBG Vietnam Series 2026 disappeared from the official schedule.

### The Financial Loss

KRAFTON suffered a loss of approximately 24 trillion VND (~$1 billion USD, ~1.33 trillion won, ~12.8% of market cap) due to the uninstall trend.
