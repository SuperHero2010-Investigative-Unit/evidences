# How to Sue Cursor (Collective Lawsuit)

**Prepared by:** SuperHero2010 Investigative Unit  
**Purpose:** Guide for individuals and legal professionals on building a collective lawsuit against Cursor (Anysphere, Inc.)  
**Status:** Public Resource, Free to Use

## 1. Overview

Cursor (Anysphere, Inc.) has been documented engaging in systematic deceptive practices against its users, particularly free-tier users. This document outlines the legal violations, the evidence supporting them, and a step-by-step guide for building a collective lawsuit.

**Target:** Anysphere, Inc. (Cursor)  
**Jurisdiction:** United States (Federal and State courts)  
**Potential Plaintiffs:** Free-tier users, Pro users affected by hidden limits, and any user harmed by deceptive practices.

---

## 2. Legal Violations

### 2.1. Fraudulent Misrepresentation

**Violation:** Cursor has made false claims about its free tier, including "free access" and "unlimited usage", while enforcing hidden limits.

**Evidence:**
- CSV logs showing message limits dropping from 14 to 1 over 8 months.
- Video recordings showing 1 message → 31-day lockout.
- Screenshots of Cursor's marketing materials.

**Legal Basis:**
- Common law fraud.
- State consumer protection laws (e.g., California Business & Professions Code § 17200).

---

### 2.2. Unfair and Deceptive Trade Practices

**Violation:** Cursor has engaged in deceptive practices, including silent cuts, hidden meters, and gaslighting emails.

**Evidence:**
- Hidden meter that is inaccessible through the normal UI.
- Email: "You were setting up Pro but didn't finish", a false narrative.
- No announcement of limit changes.

**Legal Basis:**
- Federal Trade Commission Act (Section 5), Unfair or deceptive acts or practices.
- State consumer protection laws.

---

### 2.3. Breach of Good Faith and Fair Dealing

**Violation:** Cursor has changed its policies and limits without adequate notice, undermining user trust.

**Evidence:**
- CSV logs showing limit changes without announcement.
- Email communications that gaslight users.
- Hidden meter and dashboard.

**Legal Basis:**
- Breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing (contract law).
- Violation of consumer protection laws.

---

### 2.4. Violation of Data Privacy Laws (Potential)

**Violation:** Cursor's data usage policies are ambiguous, potentially violating privacy laws.

**Evidence:**
- TOS analysis showing opt-in for training is unclear.
- Lack of clear opt-out instructions.

**Legal Basis:**
- California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA).
- General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), if applicable to EU users.

---

### 2.5. Unconscionable Contract Terms

**Violation:** Cursor's Terms of Service contain terms that are one-sided and unconscionable, including the liability cap ($100) and class action waiver.

**Evidence:**
- TOS analysis showing the $100 liability cap.
- Class action waiver.

**Legal Basis:**
- Doctrine of unconscionability (contract law).
- State consumer protection laws.

## 4. How to Build a Collective Lawsuit

### Step 1: Gather Plaintiffs

**Action:** Identify and recruit individuals who have experienced similar harms.

**Requirements:**
- Users who were blocked after 1-14 messages.
- Users who received misleading emails.
- Users who were unable to find the meter.

**How to Find Plaintiffs:**
- Share this document on social media.
- Post on Reddit (r/programming, r/technology, r/assholedesign).
- Use the SuperHero2010 Investigative Unit repository as a central hub.

---

### Step 2: Organize Evidence

**Action:** Collect and organize all evidence in a single location.

**Template Structure:**

---

### Step 3: Draft the Complaint

**Action:** Write a legal complaint outlining the violations and evidence.

**Key Sections:**
1. **Parties:** Identify plaintiffs and defendant (Anysphere, Inc.).
2. **Jurisdiction and Venue:** Federal court (diversity jurisdiction) or state court.
3. **Factual Allegations:** Describe Cursor's deceptive practices using the evidence.
4. **Legal Claims:** List the violations (see Section 2).
5. **Class Action Allegations:** Demonstrate that the case meets class action requirements (numerosity, commonality, typicality, adequacy).
6. **Prayer for Relief:** Request damages, injunctive relief, and attorneys' fees.

---

### Step 4: File the Lawsuit

**Action:** File the complaint in a court of competent jurisdiction.

**Recommended Courts:**
- United States District Court (Northern District of California), Cursor is based in San Francisco.
- State court in California.

**Filing Requirements:**
- Filing fee (approx. $400).
- Service of process on Anysphere, Inc.

---

### Step 5: Notify Potential Class Members

**Action:** Notify other affected users about the lawsuit.

**Methods:**
- Social media posts.
- Posts on Reddit and other forums.
- Collaboration with the SuperHero2010 Investigative Unit.

---

### Step 6: Discovery

**Action:** Request documents and data from Cursor.

**Key Discovery Targets:**
- Internal communications about limit changes.
- Data on user lockouts.
- Marketing materials.
- User data usage policies.

---

### Step 7: Class Certification

**Action:** File a motion to certify the class.

**Requirements:**
- Numerosity: Many users affected.
- Commonality: Common questions of law and fact.
- Typicality: Claims of representative plaintiffs are typical of the class.
- Adequacy: Plaintiffs will fairly and adequately protect the class.

---

### Step 8: Settlement or Trial

**Action:** Negotiate a settlement or proceed to trial.

**Possible Outcomes:**
- Settlement with Cursor (compensation, policy changes).
- Trial verdict in favor of plaintiffs.
- Appeal by either party.
