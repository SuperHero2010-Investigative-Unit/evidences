# Collective Reporting: A User Advocacy Strategy

## 1. Introduction

Collective reporting is a strategy where multiple users submit reports about the same issue to platform administrators, moderators, or staff. The goal is to draw attention to a problem that has been ignored or dismissed by individual reports.

**The principle is simple:**
- One report is a complaint.
- Many reports are a pattern.
- A flood of reports is a crisis.

When the volume of reports reaches a critical mass, platform staff and administrators are forced to acknowledge the issue and respond. This isn't about harassment or brigading, it's about using the existing reporting system to its full capacity.

---

## 2. Why Collective Reporting Works

| Factor | Explanation |
|--------|-------------|
| **Numbers** | A flood of reports is undeniable |
| **Visibility** | Staff can't ignore a visible pattern |
| **Accountability** | Public pressure forces a response |
| **Legitimacy** | Many voices carry more weight than one |

**Key Insight:** Platforms rely on reporting systems to identify problems. If the system is functioning properly, a large volume of reports about the same issue should trigger a review. If the system isn't functioning properly, collective reporting can expose that failure.

---

## 3. When to Use Collective Reporting

| Scenario | Appropriate? |
|----------|--------------|
| **Systematic rule violations** | Yes, when staff aren't enforcing existing rules |
| **Inconsistent enforcement** | Yes, when rules are applied unevenly |
| **Ignored complaints** | Yes, when individual reports are dismissed |
| **Abuse of power** | Yes, when staff misuse their authority |
| **Personal grievances** | No, this isn't for personal disputes |
| **Harassment** | No, this isn't for targeting individuals |

---

## 4. How to Organize Collective Reporting

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| **1** | Identify a clear, documented issue |
| **2** | Collect evidence (screenshots, URLs, timestamps) |
| **3** | Share the evidence with others |
| **4** | Ask others to submit their own reports |
| **5** | Maintain professionalism: focus on facts, not emotions |

---

## 5. What to Avoid

| Mistake | Why It Hurts |
|---------|--------------|
| **Harassment** | Targeting individuals undermines credibility |
| **Spam** | Flooding the system with low-quality reports |
| **Emotional language** | Facts are more effective than anger |
| **Coordinated brigading** | Organized vote manipulation is prohibited |
| **Off-platform coordination** | Keep actions within platform rules |

---

## 6. Potential Outcomes

| Outcome | Likelihood |
|---------|------------|
| **Staff acknowledge the issue** | High, if the volume is sufficient |
| **Staff take corrective action** | Medium, depends on the severity |
| **Staff dismiss the reports** | Low, if evidence is strong |
| **Staff retaliate** | Low, if reports are legitimate |

---

## 7. The Honest Truth

Collective reporting isn't a guarantee of change. It's a tool. Like any tool, it must be used correctly.

**Key Takeaway:**
- One report is a signal.
- Many reports are a movement.
- A flood of reports is undeniable.

When the numbers are large enough, platforms are forced to respond. They can't ignore a flood of reports. They can't dismiss a chorus of voices. They must listen.

---

## 8. Conclusion

Collective reporting is a legitimate strategy for user advocacy. It relies on the existing reporting system and uses it to its full capacity. When used responsibly, it can expose systemic issues and force platforms to address them.

**Remember:** The goal is accountability, not chaos. The goal is fairness, not revenge. The goal is change, not destruction.
