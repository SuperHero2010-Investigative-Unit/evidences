# Analysis of the Image-Line [Privacy Policy](https://www.image-line.com/privacy-policy)

## 1. GDPR Compliance Commitment

**Clause:**
> *"We have always had a robust and effective data protection programs in place which complies with existing law and abides by the data protection principles."*

**Analysis:**
Image-Line claims to be fully GDPR compliant. However, the Terms of Use analysis showed that Image-Line reserves the right to require **government-issued identification** to verify account ownership, which could be seen as excessive data collection. This Privacy Policy suggests they are transparent, but the Terms of Use reveal potential overreach.

## 2. Data Subject Access Requests (DSAR)

**Clause:**
> *"There will be no fee charged for a Data Subject Access request and any request will be responded to within one month of receipt."*

**Analysis:**
This is a standard GDPR compliance statement. However, the Terms of Use state that Image-Line "won't grant access to any account unless the requesting party can be positively identified as the rightful owner". This could create a circular problem: to access your data, you must provide additional personal data.

## 3. Data Breach Reporting

**Clause:**
> *"Image Line... will report any data breach to the data protection commissioner, within 72 hours of becoming aware of any such breach and inform any affected individuals."*

**Analysis:**
This is a standard GDPR requirement. There is no issue with this clause.

## 4. No Third-Party Data Sharing

**Clause:**
> *"At no stage will we divulge to a third party any personal data relating to the Data Subject."*

**Analysis:**
This is a positive commitment. However, the Terms of Use state that Image Line may transfer personal data to successors or assigns in the event of an assignment of rights. This is a potential loophole.

# Connection to the Case

The Privacy Policy is **less relevant** to the "scam free users" case than the Terms of Use or the Trial Exit page. It doesn't mention the trial restriction or the lock-in tactic. However, it does provide context:

1. **Data Collection**: Image-Line collects personal data, and the Terms of Use give them broad powers to verify account ownership.
2. **Account Access**: The combination of the Privacy Policy and Terms of Use creates a potential risk: users may be locked out of their accounts (and projects) if they can't provide sufficient identification.
