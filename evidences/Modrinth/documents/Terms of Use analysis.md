# [Terms of Use](https://modrinth.com/legal/terms) ANALYSIS: Modrinth

**SuperHero2010 Investigative Unit**

---

## Executive Summary

Modrinth's Terms of Use grant the company broad, unilateral power to moderate content and terminate accounts for almost any reason. However, my investigation reveals that while Modrinth may have the *right* to act poorly, they are acting *unfairly* and *inconsistently* with their own stated policies and values.

This analysis examines the relevant sections of Modrinth's Terms of Use in relation to my experience.

---

## 1. Key Provisions

### 1.1. Account Disablement

**Provision:**

> *"We have the right to disable any user name, password or other identifier... at any time in our sole discretion for any or no reason, including if, in our opinion, you have violated any provision of these Terms of Use."*

**Analysis:**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Can Modrinth suspend my account? | Yes |
| Do they need to prove a violation? | No |
| Can they suspend "for any or no reason"? | Yes |
| Is this standard for platforms? | Yes, but it is still a power imbalance |

**Implication:**

Modrinth can suspend accounts without evidence, without warning, and without justification. This is legal but creates a system where creators have no recourse.

---

### 1.2. Content Removal and Suspension

**Provision:**

> *"We have the right to: Remove or refuse to post any User Contributions for any or no reason at our sole discretion. ... Terminate or suspend your access to all or part of the Service for any or no reason, including without limitation, any violation of these Terms of Use."*

**Analysis:**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Can Modrinth remove my project? | Yes |
| Can they refuse to approve it? | Yes |
| Do they need a valid reason? | No |
| Can they act "for any or no reason"? | Yes |

**Implication:**

Modrinth can reject projects without justification. This explains how my datapack was withheld for 9+ months without a valid explanation.

---

### 1.3. Content Rules

**Provision:**

> *"All Gaming Content and User Contributions must abide by the Content Rules."*

**Analysis:**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Are Content Rules part of the Terms? | Yes |
| Are they legally binding? | Yes |
| Are they clearly defined? | No |

**Implication:**

The Content Rules are broad and vague, giving Modrinth the ability to interpret them arbitrarily. This is how they invented a versioning rule that doesn't exist.

**Analysis:**

| Question | Answer |

## 1.5. Limitation on Liability

**Provision:**

> *"IN NO EVENT WILL THE COMPANY... BE LIABLE FOR DAMAGES OF ANY KIND... INCLUDING ANY DIRECT, INDIRECT, SPECIAL, INCIDENTAL, CONSEQUENTIAL OR PUNITIVE DAMAGES..."*

**Analysis:**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Can I sue Modrinth for damages? | Yes, but the Terms attempt to limit liability |
| Are they liable for lost revenue? | They claim no, but a court may decide otherwise |
| Are they liable for emotional distress? | They claim no, but a court may decide otherwise |
| Does this clause always hold up in court? | No. Courts don't always enforce such clauses, especially if they are deemed unconscionable or if a company acts in bad faith |
| Can Modrinth ignore the law? | No. Terms can't override applicable law |

**Implication:**

Modrinth's Limitation on Liability clause is a contractual shield, not an absolute legal defense. It is designed to discourage lawsuits, but it doesn't guarantee that a court would enforce it. If Modrinth's conduct is found to be unlawful, fraudulent, or in bad faith, a court could disregard or limit the enforceability of this clause.

### The Gravity of Lawsuits

| Reality | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| Lawsuits are serious | They involve legal scrutiny, discovery, and potential penalties |
| Courts decide | A judge or jury ultimately determines liability, not Modrinth |
| Bad faith matters | If Modrinth acted in bad faith, the limitation clause may be weakened |
| Public interest | Cases involving platform behavior and creator rights can attract attention |

### Why This Matters for My Case

| Point | Explanation |
|-------|-------------|
| I am not filing a lawsuit | My goal is public documentation, not legal action |
| The clause is a deterrent | It is designed to make users think they can't sue |
| But it isn't absolute | Courts can and do reject such clauses in cases of bad faith |
| My evidence documents bad faith | Broken promises, invented rules, and silence are evidence of unfair conduct |

**My Verdict:**

> *"Modrinth's Limitation on Liability clause is a powerful deterrent, but it isn't an absolute legal shield. Courts don't always enforce such clauses, especially when a company acts in bad faith. While I am not pursuing legal action, my evidence documents a pattern of unfair conduct that could undermine Modrinth's contractual protections in any serious legal scrutiny."*
