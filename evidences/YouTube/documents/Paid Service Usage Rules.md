### 2.1. Rental Content: Limited Viewing Period

**The Clause:**
*"When you rent content, you may view such item of content an unlimited number of times during the period of time displayed at the time of your payment ("Viewing Period")... Pausing, stopping, or rewinding an item of rented content will not extend your applicable Viewing Period."*

**Severity: EXTREME. This is a ticking clock.**

You pay for a rental, but the clock doesn't stop. If you pause, stop, or rewind, you lose time. **YouTube is punishing you for having a life.** If you have an emergency, you lose your rental. This isn't customer service. This is a trap.

**Why this is illegal:**
- **Unfair Contract Terms:** This clause is punitive and one-sided
- **Consumer Protection Violations:** You are paying for a service that doesn't respect your time
- **Deceptive Practice:** You are led to believe you are renting content, but you are actually buying a countdown

**The Mass Lawsuit:** Millions of users have lost rental time due to this policy. A class action could force YouTube to provide more reasonable viewing periods.

### 2.2. Purchased Content: Locker Period

**The Clause:**
*"The 'Locker Period' will be for at least 5 years from the date of your purchase... if an item of purchased content becomes unavailable during the five year period from the purchase date, you may request a refund."*

**Severity: EXTREME. This is a limited license, not ownership.**

You don't own content you purchase on YouTube. **You are renting it for up to five years.** After that, YouTube can take it away and offer you a "partial refund". This isn't ownership. This is a time-limited lease.

**Why this is illegal:**
- **Deceptive Practice:** You are led to believe you are purchasing content, but you are only renting it
- **Unfair Contract Terms:** This clause is one-sided and oppressive
- **Consumer Protection Violations:** Many laws prohibit such "revocable" purchases

**The Mass Lawsuit:** Users have lost thousands of dollars worth of content. A class action could force YouTube to provide permanent access to purchased content.

### 2.3. Purchased Content: Streaming Limitations

**The Clause:**
- *"you may view one stream of each item at a time,"*
- *"you may view up to 3 streams of different items at a time,"*
- *"you may authorize up to 5 devices for offline playback... and to authorize additional devices, you must deauthorize one of those 5 devices,"*
- *"you may only authorize the same device three times in any 12 month period and de-authorize the same device twice in any 12 month period,"*
- *"you may only deauthorize a total of 2 devices for offline playback every 90 days,"*
- *"you may only authorize 3 Google accounts on the same device."*

**Severity: EXTREME. This is a prison of arbitrary limits.**

You cannot watch your own content on more than one device at a time. You cannot authorize more than five devices. You cannot deauthorize devices more than a few times per year. **YouTube is treating you like a pirate, not a customer.**

**Why this is illegal:**
- **Unfair Contract Terms:** These limits are arbitrary and punitive
- **Consumer Protection Violations:** You are being restricted from using content you paid for
- **Anti-Consumer Practice:** These limits are designed to restrict usage, not to protect rights

**The Mass Lawsuit:** Users are being unfairly restricted from accessing their own content. A class action could force YouTube to remove or reduce these limits.
