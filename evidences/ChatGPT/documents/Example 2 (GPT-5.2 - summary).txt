In my game project, which was inspired by "Where's my Water?", I showed ChatGPT the properties of WMW and their replacement variables in the game. Initially, it understood and analyzed correctly. Next, I asked it to implement a feature "choose one of two tools, a shovel or a pickaxe, to cut sand or rock". At this point, it started making constant errors. When focusing on the list of tools (which contained many different mineral types), it provided a lot of incorrect and irrelevant code, causing the game to not work as expected. Later, I had to ask it for a different approach. When implemented, all the code worked effectively except for the cutting behavior. When I tried cutting sand with a shovel, almost no sand pixels were removed! When I tried cutting rock with a pickaxe, there was no clear cutting radius, just random cutting within a radius! I asked for fixes 10 times, but it kept failing because it kept guessing. I was forced to resend the file for it to read. At this point, the problem was solved. However, when it came to smoothing the pixels (converting pixel ladders into sliders), it completely failed. It confidently used methods like alpha, neighbor, and 2D pollygon collider. But in practice: alpha caused extremely rapid durability degradation; neighbor was completely pixel-like; and the 2D pollygon collider caused the liquid to stand on air (the transparent part of the image) due to the removal of the 2D box collider. I criticized it and reverted to the original pixel. This time I gave up.
