# Adobe [General Terms of Use](https://adobe.com/vn_en/legal/terms.html): Analysis

- **Document:** General Terms of Use
- **Company:** Adobe Inc.
- **Last Updated:** June 18, 2024
- **Analyzed by:** SuperHero2010 Investigative Unit

## 1. Overview
This document analyzes the Adobe General Terms of Use (General TOU) to identify clauses relevant to my investigation with a legal framework that heavily favors Adobe, with broad user rights granted, extensive liability disclaimers, and significant control retained by Adobe.

---

## 2. My Key Findings (Critical Traps for Users)

This section highlights the clauses I find most concerning for users.

### 2.1 Broad License to Your Content (Section 4.3)

This is the most crucial part of the agreement. While Adobe states, "we don't train generative AI models on your content" (with the exception of Adobe Stock), I see that they still require a **very broad, non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free license** to your Cloud Content. This license is necessary for them to provide their services, but I note the vast scope of rights they claim:

- **Reproduce:** They can make copies of my work on their servers
- **Distribute:** They can publish and share my work at my direction
- **Create derivative works:** They can compress, crop, or translate my content
- **Publicly display/perform:** They can show my work on their platforms (like Behance) at my direction
- **Sublicense:** They can allow their trusted infrastructure providers to access my content

**My Concern:** While the stated purpose is "to operate the Services and Software on your behalf", I consider the scope of this license to be overly broad and permissive. I am granting Adobe rights far beyond simply storing my files, and these rights are granted without any additional compensation.

### 2.2 Content Analytics (Section 4.3(B))

This grants Adobe the right to perform "Content Analytics", which I understand means analyzing my Cloud Content to understand how I use their services and improve them. This includes file type and structural attributes.

**My Concern:** This is **opt-out** by default. I must actively navigate to a separate webpage to disable this. I find it deceptive that a data analysis practice of this nature isn't an explicit opt-in.

### 2.3 Generative AI Loophole (Sections 2.2(F) & 4.3)

Adobe repeatedly states: *"We don't train generative AI models on your or your customers’ content unless you’ve submitted the content to the Adobe Stock marketplace."*

**My Concern:** This appears to be a carefully worded loophole. It doesn't apply to users who are Adobe Stock contributors. By submitting content to Adobe Stock, users are explicitly agreeing to have their work used for generative AI training under a separate agreement. The ToS also doesn't prevent future changes to this policy.

### 2.4 Indemnification Clause (Section 8.2)

This is a significant trap. I would be required to **indemnify** (legally defend and pay for) Adobe against any claims or damages arising from my use of their services, my content, or my violation of the Terms.

**My Concern:** This places a massive legal and financial burden on me as a user. It's a one-sided protection for Adobe and a significant risk for me, even if I believe a claim against Adobe is without merit.

### 2.5 Limitation of Liability (Section 10): The Shield of Impunity

**Section 10.1: Total Exclusion of Liability**

Adobe disclaims all liability for any "special, incidental, indirect, consequential... damages". In plain language, this means that if their software deletes my entire year's work, corrupts my project files, or causes me to lose a client and my reputation, I can't claim any compensation for those losses. This isn't a limitation. This is a total abdication of responsibility.

**Why This Is Illegal:**

- **Unconscionable Under U.S. Law:** A contract term that strips a party of all meaningful remedy is considered unconscionable. Courts routinely strike down such clauses when they are one-sided and oppressive
- **Violation of Implied Warranty:** Adobe sells software "AS-IS", attempting to disclaim even the implied warranty of merchantability (that the software will work for its intended purpose). Under the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC), such disclaimers must be conspicuous and explicit. Adobe buries them in dense legalese
- **Consumer Protection Violations:** In the EU, Australia, and many U.S. states, consumer protection laws prohibit companies from excluding liability for failures that cause significant harm. These clauses are often void as a matter of public policy

**Section 10.2: The $100 Cap**

Adobe's total liability is capped at the greater of **US $100** or **the amount I paid in the last three months**. Let me be clear: if I pay $600 a year for Creative Cloud, and Adobe's software deletes a project I have worked on for months, worth tens of thousands of dollars, my maximum compensation is $100. This isn't a fair limitation. This is a deliberate act of corporate protectionism.

**Why This Is Illegal:**

- **Disproportionate and Unfair:** A $100 cap on liability for a service that costs hundreds of dollars per year is grossly disproportionate to the potential harm. It's an unfair contract term under EU and UK law, and may be void under the Unfair Contract Terms Act
- **No Meaningful Remedy:** Users have no real recourse. They are forced to accept a product that may fail catastrophically, with no financial protection. This encourages Adobe to neglect quality and security, knowing they will never face meaningful consequences
- **Violates Basic Justice:** A company can't sell a product for a high price and then claim it owes nothing if that product destroys the user's livelihood. This isn't a contract. This is a protection racket

**The True Gravity:**

Adobe's limitation of liability isn't a neutral clause. It's a calculated strategy to evade accountability. It sends a clear message: *"We take your money, but we take no responsibility for your work."*. If a critical bug wipes out months of creative work, Adobe will offer $100 and walk away. This is legalized theft.

### 2.6 Mandatory Arbitration and Class Action Waiver (Section 14): The Muzzle of Justice

**Section 14: Removal of Rights**

This clause forces users to resolve any dispute through individual, confidential arbitration. It also strips users of their right to join a class-action lawsuit. This isn't a choice. This is a forced surrender of fundamental legal rights. Users can't sue Adobe in court. They can't join forces with others who have been harmed. They must fight alone, in secret, at a cost most can't afford.

**Why This Is Illegal:**

- **Denial of Access to Justice:** Individual arbitration is expensive. Filing fees, arbitrator costs, and legal representation can cost thousands of dollars. Most users can't afford this. They are effectively barred from seeking justice. This is a deliberate tactic to silence users
- **Conceals Systemic Harm:** By forcing arbitration and banning class actions, Adobe ensures that widespread failures never come to light. If 10,000 users lose their work due to a bug, Adobe faces only 10,000 individual arbitration cases, not a single class action. The scale of the harm is hidden. The public never knows. Adobe never pays
- **Unconscionable as a Contract Term:** Courts have repeatedly struck down mandatory arbitration clauses when they are hidden, one-sided, or oppressive. In many jurisdictions, such clauses are void if they aren't clearly and conspicuously presented. Adobe buries this clause in dense legal language, with a mere 30-day window to opt out
- **Violates the Right to a Jury Trial:** The Seventh Amendment to the U.S. Constitution protects the right to a jury trial. Forced arbitration waives this right. Users don't knowingly or willingly give up this fundamental right, it's taken from them through fine print

**The 30-Day Opt-Out Trap:**

Adobe allows users to opt out of arbitration, but only within 30 days of first using the software. Most users don't read the terms. Most users don't know about this window. By the time they are harmed, the window is closed. This isn't a choice. This is a trap.

**The True Gravity:**

Adobe's mandatory arbitration and class action waiver isn't a neutral clause. It's a muzzle. It silences users. It hides harm. It ensures that Adobe will never face the consequences of its failures. It's a weapon used against users, designed to protect Adobe from accountability.

### 2.7 Account and Content Termination (Sections 3.3 & 11)

**Section 3.3 & 11:** If I cancel my subscription (or if Adobe terminates it), I have only **30 days** to download my Content before Adobe reserves the right to delete it.

**My Concern:** For users with massive libraries or active projects, 30 days can be a short timeframe. This is a powerful lever Adobe holds, as it forces users to carefully manage their departure. Furthermore, if they terminate my account for "cause" (Section 11.2), I have no right to access my Content at all.

### 2.8 Business Email Domains Risk (Section 1.4)

If I create an Adobe account using a business email address, that Business can potentially take control of my account and all the Content within it, even if I consider it personal.

**My Concern:** This is a clear and intentional trap for employees. Many users likely sign up for free trials or use personal projects on work accounts, unaware that their employer can take ownership of that data at any time.

### 2.9 Free Account Inactivity (Section 5.3)

If I have a free Adobe account and don't sign in periodically, Adobe can permanently delete my Content or close my account entirely after attempting to provide notice.

**My Concern:** Users with legacy free accounts could lose years of work or access to important project files simply due to inactivity.

### 2.10 Disclaimers of Warranties (Section 9)

The services are provided **"AS-IS"**. Adobe disclaims all warranties that the services will be "uninterrupted, timely, secure, or error-free", or that the "results... will be effective, accurate, or reliable".

**My Concern:** This is a total disclaimer of responsibility for the quality, security, and availability of their paid services. I am paying for a service they clearly state they don't guarantee.

## 4. My Conclusion

**I conclude that the Adobe General Terms of Use are a one-sided legal document written to protect Adobe, not the user.** While they appear to respect user ownership of content, the required license is extremely broad, and the clauses on liability, indemnification, and dispute resolution are heavily tilted in Adobe's favor.

The most significant findings I have documented are the **broad license** on user content, the **opt-out Content Analytics**, the **generative AI loophole**, the **indemnification requirement**, the **$100 liability cap**, and the **mandatory arbitration clause**.

In my opinion, by agreeing to these terms, a user is granting Adobe extensive rights while accepting a very low level of responsibility and accountability from the company. This is consistent with the pattern I have documented for other major technology companies.
