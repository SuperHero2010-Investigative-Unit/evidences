# Article Analysis 1: [Adobe Account Lockout](https://m.cafef.vn/nguoi-dung-viet-dung-adobe-ban-quyen-van-bi-khoa-tai-khoan-188260625133820064.chn)

## 1. Article Overview

- **Title:** Vietnamese users with licensed Adobe accounts still get locked out
- **Source:** CafeF.vn (via Đời Sống Pháp Luật)
- **Date:** June 25, 2026
- **My Initial Assessment:** This article documents a pattern of behavior I have seen before with other tech companies: automated "fraud detection" systems that falsely flag legitimate users, causing financial loss and wasted time, with minimal accountability or recourse from the company

---

## 2. My Critical Findings

### 2.1. The "Fraud Detection" System is a Black Box

The article describes the **Adobe Genuine Service (AGS)** as a background process that "remembers" a device's history, even if pirated software was previously installed and since removed. I find this to be an invasive and punitive measure. It creates a permanent "digital fingerprint" that Adobe can use to justify terminating a paid account, regardless of a user's current compliance. This isn't anti-piracy, it's retroactive punishment for past actions, which I consider fundamentally unfair.

### 2.2. The Three Groups of Affected Users

The article identifies three groups, and I note that **two of them aren't at fault in Adobe's eyes:**

- **Group 1 (Past Pirate Users):** Users who have since purchased a legitimate license. Adobe's system still flags them based on past history. I consider this a clear injustice
- **Group 2 (Compromised Accounts):** Users who unknowingly purchased illegitimate accounts from third-party resellers. I see them as victims of fraud, not perpetrators
- **Group 3 (False Positives):** Users with no history of violation who are wrongly flagged. Adobe admits this is a possibility

**My Concern:** Adobe's system is designed to identify "fraud", but I interpret it as a blunt instrument that penalizes historical behavior and unknowing victims, not just active bad actors.

### 2.3. The "No Refund" Policy

The article reminds me that the General Terms of Use allow Adobe to terminate accounts and **retain prepaid fees** if they claim a violation. I identify this as a powerful financial incentive for Adobe to aggressively enforce, even if it means punishing innocent users. It shifts all risk to the user.

### 2.4. User Recourse is Costly and Uncertain

The recommended solutions are:

- **Contact Adobe Support:** I have documented similar experiences with other companies where support is unhelpful or defensive. I suspect Adobe's support may be no different
- **Chargeback via Bank:** This is the only potentially effective remedy, but it damages the user's relationship with Adobe and isn't a guaranteed solution

---

## 3. My Analysis of the Evidence

I see this article as a case study in corporate behavior that is consistent with the patterns I have documented across other AI and software companies.

| Pattern I Have Documented | How This Article Confirms It |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Defensive & Opaque Systems** | Adobe uses a secretive, automated "fraud" system (AGS) without clear transparency or avenues for appeal |
| **Shifting Blame to the User** | The system targets users for past behavior or unknowing purchases of illegitimate licenses, blaming them for "fraud" |
| **Neglecting Bug Fixes & User Support** | Adobe acknowledges "false positives" but hasn't provided a quick and simple appeals process, instead forcing users to go through support |
| **Forcing Upgrades or Financial Harm** | Users lose access to their work and prepaid fees, effectively forcing them to either accept the loss or fight for a chargeback |
| **Intentionally Broken Design** | The AGS system itself appears designed to flag and terminate accounts with minimal human oversight, causing harm to innocent users |

---

## 4. My Conclusion

**I conclude that this article provides strong evidence of a corporate policy that prioritizes aggressive enforcement and profit protection over fair treatment of its paying users.** The Adobe Genuine Service (AGS) is, in my view, a tool for retroactive punishment and proactive termination based on a flawed system. Adobe appears to be shifting the financial and legal risk of its anti-piracy efforts onto its user base, including those who have acted in good faith.

This incident reveals a fundamental flaw in the relationship between the company and its customers: Adobe creates a system where even legitimate, paying users can be arbitrarily locked out of their accounts and their work with little to no recourse, and the company profits from their continued subscription fees.

This is a critical piece of evidence that aligns with the broader pattern of deceptive and user-hostile practices I have been investigating.

# Article Analysis 2: [The "Cheap Adobe" Trap](https://m.kenh14.vn/sap-bay-adobe-gia-re-gioi-sang-tao-noi-dung-khoc-rong-vi-tai-khoan-bay-mau-sat-gio-nop-bai-215260802153601618.chn)

## 1. Article Overview

- **Title:** Falling for the 'Cheap Adobe' trap: Content creators cry as accounts vanish right before deadlines
- **Source:** Kenh14.vn
- **Date:** August 3, 2026
- **My Initial Assessment:** This article highlights a predatory ecosystem where third-party sellers exploit both Adobe's complex licensing and users' desire for affordable tools. I see it as a cautionary tale that, while warning users, also inadvertently reveals the high cost of Adobe's official pricing and the harsh consequences of their account termination policies

---

## 2. My Critical Findings

### 2.1. The "Too Good to Be True" Scam

The article details a classic scam: third-party sellers offer Adobe subscriptions at a fraction of the official price (e.g., 1/10th the cost), often using stolen credit cards or illegitimate educational licenses. I note that the sellers promise "lifetime warranty", a clear red flag that should signal the offer is fraudulent.

### **My Concern:** I see this as a perfect storm. Adobe's official pricing is high, creating a massive demand for cheaper alternatives. Scammers fill this void, and when their fraudulent accounts are inevitably shut down by Adobe, the end-user (the creator) is the one who suffers the consequences, not the scammer. The user loses their money, their access to the software, and crucially, their work stored in the cloud.

### 2.2. The Devastating Consequence: Data Loss

The article states that when these accounts are terminated, **"toàn bộ file làm việc lưu trên Cloud bị xóa sạch không thể khôi phục"** (all work files stored on the Cloud are completely deleted and can't be recovered). I find this to be a catastrophic outcome, especially for professionals or students working on time-sensitive projects. This isn't just a financial loss, it's a loss of intellectual property and hours of work.

### 2.3. The Unhelpful "Solution"

The article ends by advising users to **"say no to floating software"** and to buy only from **"official distributors."**

**My Concern:** I see this as a victim-blaming conclusion. While avoiding scams is wise, this advice doesn't address the underlying issues I have identified:

- **Adobe's High Pricing:** The official pricing is a barrier for many, especially students and freelancers, driving them to seek alternatives in the first place
- **Adobe's Harsh Enforcement:** I view the immediate, irreversible deletion of all cloud data without warning or appeal as a disproportionate and cruel policy, even when a user is found to be in violation (often unknowingly). This is an example of a broader pattern of a punitive, non-transparent system

---

## 3. My Analysis of the Evidence

I consider this article further proof of the harmful consequences of the "user-hostile" corporate model I have been documenting. It shows a three-way failure:

| The Failure | My Observation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Predatory Scammers** | Exploit users' financial needs and Adobe's complex licensing |
| **Adobe's Punitive System** | They don't simply deactivate the account, they **permanently delete the user's content without warning**. I believe this is a disproportionate response designed to inflict maximum pain and discourage future violations, but it also harms innocent users who may have been victims of fraud |
| **The User's Plight** | Users are caught in the middle, losing money and work. They are often unaware they are even violating the terms until it's too late |

---

## 4. My Conclusion

**I conclude that this article, while intended as a consumer warning, also reveals the severe and disproportionate consequences of Adobe's punitive policies.** The article shows that even if a user isn't the actual fraudster, they can lose not just their software access, but their entire body of cloud-based work. This is an extreme penalty that I see as a strong-arm tactic used by Adobe to enforce its ecosystem.

The advice to "only buy from official sources" is, in my view, an insufficient solution. It ignores the primary driver of these scams: Adobe's own pricing, which is inaccessible to many, and its draconian enforcement policies that punish users without due process. I see this as a clear example of a company using its power to create a hostile environment where users are constantly at risk of losing their work and their investment with little to no warning or fair appeal.

# Article Analysis 4: ["Warning: Buying Cheap Adobe Software Puts Businesses at Risk of Total Data Loss"](https://thanhnien.vn/canh-bao-tinh-trang-mua-phan-mem-adobe-gia-re-khien-doanh-nghiep-mat-trang-du-lieu-185260809180114323.htm)

## 1. Article Overview

- **Title:** Warning: Buying cheap Adobe software puts businesses at risk of total data loss
- **Source:** Thanh Nien
- **Date:** August 10, 2026
- **My Initial Assessment:** This article warns businesses about the risks of buying cheap Adobe licenses from unauthorized resellers. I see this as another example of the pattern I have documented: Adobe's punitive enforcement system harms users, and the blame is shifted entirely onto the victims

---

## 2. My Critical Findings

### 2.1. The "Victim Blaming" Narrative

The article frames the issue as a user error: businesses are buying cheap licenses and suffering the consequences.

**My Concern:** I see this as a classic victim-blaming narrative. While I acknowledge that businesses should be cautious, I believe the article fails to hold Adobe accountable for its own actions. The real problem isn't that users are buying cheap licenses; it's that Adobe has created a system where users are at constant risk, and where legitimate, paying users can be locked out without warning. The article doesn't address the fundamental unfairness of Adobe's enforcement system, which I have documented extensively.

### 2.2. The Consequence: Total Data Loss

The article states that when an account is terminated, **"toàn bộ tài nguyên lưu trữ trên nền tảng đám mây, các dự án đang triển khai của doanh nghiệp sẽ bị xóa sạch"** (all cloud storage resources and ongoing projects of the business will be completely deleted).

**My Concern:** I find this to be an extreme and disproportionate punishment. A business that unknowingly purchases an illegitimate license loses not just access but also all of its work. This isn't a reasonable enforcement measure, it's a form of corporate violence against users. The article presents this as a natural consequence, but I see it as a deliberate, punitive policy designed to inflict maximum damage and deter violations.

### 2.3. The "Official Channel" Solution is Inadequate

The article advises businesses to buy only through official channels.

**My Concern:** I consider this an insufficient solution. While it's good advice, it doesn't address the root causes I have identified:

- **High Official Prices:** Adobe's official pricing is inaccessible to many businesses, driving them to seek alternatives
- **Punitive Enforcement:** Even legitimate users can be locked out, as I have documented
- **No Real Recourse:** Businesses have no meaningful way to appeal a termination or recover their data, even if they aren't at fault

### 2.4. The Missing Context: Adobe's Responsibility

The article doesn't mention that:

- Adobe's official pricing is chaotic, confusing, and extremely high
- Adobe's enforcement system is opaque and has a history of false positives
- Adobe doesn't provide a meaningful appeals process
- Adobe doesn't provide official archives of older versions, forcing users to rely on unsafe sources

**My Concern:** By omitting this context, the article implicitly defends Adobe's practices. It frames the issue as a matter of user behavior, not a systemic failure by Adobe.

---

## 3. My Analysis of the Evidence

I see this article as another piece of evidence confirming the pattern I have documented across all my Adobe analyses.

## 4. My Conclusion

**I conclude that this article, while well-intentioned, is a victim-blaming piece that fails to hold Adobe accountable for its own practices.** It warns businesses about the risks of buying cheap licenses, but it doesn't question why the official pricing is so high, why the enforcement system is so punitive and opaque, or why Adobe doesn't provide a fair appeals process.

The article is evidence of a pattern I have seen repeatedly: when users are harmed by a company's policies, the company and the media shift the blame onto the users.
