## 2. My Critical Findings

### 2.1. Overwhelming Number of Options

I count at least 14 distinct plans or add-ons listed, each with a different price and a different combination of applications. I find this to be an intentional strategy to overwhelm users. With so many options, I believe the average user will struggle to compare them effectively and may end up choosing a more expensive or less suitable plan by mistake.

### 2.2. Chaotic Pricing and Inconsistencies

The pricing appears illogical and inconsistent to me:

| Plan | My Observed Price | My Concern |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Creative Cloud Pro, Stock AI Studio** | $34.99/month | High price for a combined plan; I question the value of the AI Studio component. |
| **Photography (Lr + Ps), Acrobat Pro, Firefly Pro** | $19.99/month | This seems like a value bundle, but I note it includes Firefly Pro, which is also sold separately for a different price. |
| **Firefly Premium** | $139.91/month | I consider this price shockingly high. This is a massive cost for a single AI feature. I see this as an intentional "premium" tier to make other plans seem more reasonable. |
| **Substance 3D Collection** | $59.99/month | Very expensive for a specialized set of tools. |
| **Creative Cloud Standard** | $54.99/month | This appears to be a core plan, but the price is still very high, especially when compared to the $19.99 Photography plan. |

**My Concern:** I see no clear logic to the pricing. I believe these prices are not based on the cost of providing the service but are instead optimized to extract the maximum amount from different user segments.

### 2.3. Inconsistent and Confusing Naming

The naming conventions are, in my view, a major source of confusion:

- **"Firefly Pro"** appears in multiple plans with different prices. I see no clear way to know what is included in "Firefly Pro" vs. "Firefly Pro Plus" vs. "Firefly Standard" vs. "Firefly Premium"
- **"Acrobat Pro"** and **"Acrobat Standard"** are separate, but I find the difference between them unclear
- **"Creative Cloud Pro"** and **"Creative Cloud Standard"** sound similar but have vastly different prices and, I assume, different feature sets

**My Concern:** I interpret this inconsistent naming as a deliberate tactic to make it difficult for me, as a user, to understand exactly what I am buying and to compare plans effectively.

### 2.4. The "Sell the Bundle" Strategy

I note that several plans bundle applications together (e.g., Photography, Substance 3D Collection). I see this as a strategy to force users to pay for multiple applications they may not need in order to access the one or two they actually want. This increases the overall cost and is a common tactic I've seen in other software suites.

### 2.5. Extreme AI Pricing

I am particularly struck by the pricing of the Firefly AI plans:

- **Firefly Standard:** $9.99/month
- **Firefly Pro Plus:** $34.97/month
- **Firefly Premium:** $139.91/month

I consider this a significant risk for users. I believe Adobe is using a "loss leader" strategy to get users dependent on AI features and then will increase the price or introduce new limitations over time, making it difficult to stop using the tool.

### 2.6. Inconsistent prices

#### Example
| Example | My Analysis of the Inconsistency | Why I Consider This a Problem |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Photography ($19.99) is cheaper than Lightroom ($11.99) and Photoshop ($22.99) individually.** | I find this completely illogical. A bundle of two applications is priced **between** the cost of the two individual applications. This creates a confusing scenario where a user might unintentionally pay more for less. | This is a classic decoy pricing strategy. I see it as a deliberate attempt to confuse me about the true value of each product and push me toward a bundle I may not fully need. |
| **Substance 3D Texturing ($24.99) is cheaper than Substance 3D Collection ($59.99).** | I consider this illogical because "Texturing" (Sa + Ds + Pt) is a subset of the "Collection". | I believe this is a tactic to segment the market. The cheaper "Texturing" plan is designed to hook users, who will then be upsold to the much more expensive "Collection" when they need a specific tool not included in the cheaper plan. |

#### Broader Analysis: The Inconsistency is Intentional

I now conclude that the "inconsistencies" I identified aren't mistakes; they are a calculated part of Adobe's business strategy. I see multiple clear tactics at play:

1.  **I See the "Decoy" Effect:** I believe the expensive, individual app prices (like $22.99 for just Photoshop) are designed to make the bundles (like the $19.99 Photography plan) appear to be a "great deal", even though the bundle includes multiple apps. In my view, the individual app price is intentionally inflated

2.  **I Observe Market Segmentation:** I believe Adobe is pricing its products based on what different user groups are willing to pay, not on the cost of the software. Students and hobbyists might be more likely to buy the cheaper "Photography" plan, while professionals who need the full "Collection" are forced to pay the higher price. The confusing array of "Pro", "Standard", and "Premium" tiers is, in my view, designed to segment users by their willingness to pay, not by their actual needs

3.  **I See a Strategy of "Planned Obsolescence" for Plans:** I'm highly suspicious of the new AI-based plans (Firefly Standard, Pro, Premium). I believe these are designed to get users dependent on a specific set of features, after which Adobe can increase the price or change the terms, knowing users will be "locked in" and find it difficult to stop using the tools

#### Conclusion

**I conclude that the inconsistencies in Adobe's pricing are a deliberate, calculated strategy of confusion and market segmentation, not a sign of error or complexity.**

My analysis of these prices, combined with my analysis of the Subscription and Cancellation Terms, confirms my view that Adobe's business model is built on:

- **Creating confusion** to prevent informed choices
- **Using decoy pricing** to push users toward more expensive bundles
- **Segmenting the market** to extract maximum revenue from different user types
- **Locking users in** with a confusing array of plans and a punitive cancellation policy

I see this as the logical conclusion of my investigation into Adobe's practices. The "inconsistent prices" aren't a bug; they are a core feature of a user-hostile business model designed to maximize profit at the expense of clarity and fairness for the user.
